# Test bot movement and receive targeting

Each court helper has Follow ball, Static and Move modes in F9. Follow retains immediate placement; Static keeps its designated ground location. Move travels from its existing location toward the assigned contact interception, with an independent 0–12 m/s ground-speed slider (default 6). Zero holds ground position. Tracking accuracy applies to Follow and Move; contact-order assignments remain unchanged. Slow Move bots can miss. Attack/block jump height and contact preparation retain the existing physical motion; horizontal movement including their approach obeys the Move speed cap without teleporting at plan start or landing. Settings survive lab reset.

Receivers aim toward the current position of an enabled setter on their own side. The final horizontal heading is maintained after contact geometry and timing effects. Within the calibrated receive range, the setter location is the intended destination. Beyond that range, the ball travels toward the setter without being granted extra range. With no enabled same-side setter, the previous nominal receive target remains. Opposing setters are never selected.

bot_move_test.gd verifies speed limits, missed/reached contacts, attack movement, same-side setter targeting beyond range, and actual resolved receive direction. Existing bot settings, rally, block, arc and core tests cover compatibility.

Spike orientation: court attackers turn toward their intended opposing-court shot during the grounded approach, then commit yaw at takeoff. Their shot is clamped to the same ±15 degree horizontal attack-influence limit as player DI. Torso/arm swing follows that bounded relative heading; body yaw does not track targets in flight. Pepper attacks use the same shot constraint against their pre-jump facing. Rescue swings continue along committed facing.


Aiming accuracy is independent of tracking accuracy and movement speed. F9 exposes a 0–100% slider per court helper and per Pepper partner. Defaults are 100% (no additional aiming error). Lower ratings add bounded lateral/depth error equivalent to up to 2.5 m at the nominal ballistic floor intercept, preserving vertical flight. Spike intent remains body-facing constrained before geometric contact bias; quality and Timing still affect the result even at 100%. Receiver shots keep their enabled same-side setter heading and vary depth only. Ability-free rescue spikes keep facing-only direction. Aim uses a separate RNG, and changing its slider does not replan movement or change athlete attributes. Values survive reset. bot_aim_test.gd verifies scatter, zero-error identity, actual contacts, and independent settings.


Move setters now choose an elevated contact plane (up to 0.9 m above their standing set anchor, limited by the incoming apex), and use BotMotion plus the authored jump-set pose. Attackers and Move setters have a minimum jump attempt instead of a zero-height motion plan. If no valid intercept exists, the assigned overhead role approaches a bounded projected point and attempts a jump; only actual hurtbox overlap can produce a contact. Assignment/contact-order limits still apply. Pepper setters use the same elevated preference, and Pepper overhead actions retain their intended action and attempt a jump even when the prediction is unreachable. Jump-set output uses the airborne set arc rather than the ground-set rise reduction. bot_jump_attempt_test.gd verifies airborne contacts and unsuccessful attempts without fake touches.
