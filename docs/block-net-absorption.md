# Block and net absorption tuning

Blocks resolve capacity/charge mismatch before selecting a regional deflection. A matched central block retains at most 12% of incoming speed; severe overpowering progressively raises the nominal retention to 48%. Edge contacts soften that retention further. Geometry still distinguishes controlled returns, low outward touches and upper backward touches. Flow further reduces retained pace. Direction mixing can reduce the final speed below the nominal fraction, and the response publishes both actual speed retention and absorbed speed fraction. There is no minimum rebound impulse for a stationary ball.

This uses the existing one-sided block-capacity mismatch: having capacity above the incoming charge is not a new overcharge fault. Larger incoming deficits can create either favorable or adverse deflections according to contact geometry and incoming direction, rather than guaranteeing a tool-out.

Net tape uses 55% tangential and 10% normal speed retention. Webbing uses stronger damping (38 to 14 across pressure), absorbs lateral and vertical speed on entry, and retains its stretched-pocket/release model. Gravity continues normally after contact. The shared tape helper retains its original defaults for the Transition Set basket rim; the net passes its softer tuning explicitly.

Verification: contact_absorption_test sweeps regional contacts and mismatch levels, including zero-speed input. Existing block, net, basket and bot-block regressions cover swept contacts, rally continuation, net faults and rim behavior. The manually installed passive-block test explicitly sets its intercept so it does not depend on a prior contact's flight.
